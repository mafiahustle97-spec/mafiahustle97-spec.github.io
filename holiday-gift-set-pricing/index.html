How to Price Gift Sets and Holiday Discounts Without Losing Money

December is the busiest month for most handmade shops, and the easiest one to work hard for very little. Gift sets, percentage-off codes and "free shipping" all change your profit per sale. Here's how to price each one with real numbers before you list it.

Every example uses the 8 oz soy candle from our candle pricing guide: $4.45 of materials, 15 minutes of work at $20 an hour, and 10% overhead. That's a full cost of $10.40, and a suggested Etsy price of $19.99 at a 35% target margin, which earns $7.24 a sale. Fees use US Etsy defaults (6.5% transaction, 3% + $0.25 processing, $0.20 listing); check Etsy's fee page for your country. All figures are examples, so swap in your own.

The formula you'll use for every offer

Price = (full cost + fixed fees) ÷ (1 − fee % − target margin)

With US Etsy defaults: fixed fees $0.45, fee % 9.5%. At a 35% margin the divisor is 0.555.

1. Gift sets: price the set, not the parts

A gift set isn't two products in one box. It has its own costs (a gift box, tissue, a card) and some savings (you pack once, and print one label). Cost it as a new product.

Two-candle gift setCost
Materials (2 × $4.45)$8.90
Gift box and tissue$1.20
Labour (30 min, less 3 min saved by packing once: 27 min at $20/hr)$9.00
Overhead (10%)$1.91
Full cost$21.01

Price = ($21.01 + $0.45) ÷ 0.555 = $38.67, so list it at $38.99.

At $38.99 the fees are $4.15 and the set earns $13.83, a 35% margin. Two candles bought separately cost $39.98, so the buyer saves $0.99 and gets a gift box, while you keep nearly the same profit as two single sales ($14.48). That's a set that's genuinely good for both sides.

The common mistake is "two candles at $19.99, minus 15% for the bundle". That gives $33.98, earns about $9.29 after the box and fees, $4.54 less than the $38.99 set, and turns your busiest product into your least profitable one.

2. Percentage-off codes: know how many extra sales you need

A discount comes straight out of your profit, not out of your price. Here's what happens to the $19.99 candle:

OfferPriceFeesProfit per saleSales needed to match full price
Full price$19.99$2.35$7.241.0×
10% off$17.99$2.16$5.431.3×
25% off$14.99$1.87$2.722.7×

At 25% off you need about 2.7 times as many sales just to earn the same. That can work for a clear-out of old stock. As a December default, it usually means more pouring for less money.

Better seasonal levers that don't cut your margin:

  1. A gift set priced as above.
  2. A small free extra with a real, counted cost (a matchbook, a sample melt) instead of a percentage off.
  3. An honest deadline on a modest code (for example, 10% until a fixed date), so it isn't a permanent price cut.

3. "Free shipping": build it into the price, then check it

If you absorb $5.00 of shipping, treat it as a cost:

Price = ($10.40 + $5.00 + $0.45) ÷ 0.555 = $28.56, so list it at $28.99 with free shipping.

That earns $10.39 after shipping and $3.20 of fees, still a 35% margin. If $28.99 feels too high for one candle, that's a signal to offer free shipping on the gift set instead, where $5.00 is a smaller share of the price.

4. Craft fairs and markets: price for the stall, not just the product

A December market adds a pitch fee, travel and hours on your feet. Spread them across the units you expect to sell, or track each event's real profit per hour afterwards. Our craft fair profit tracker guide shows how.

5. Stocking fillers and minis: watch the fixed fees

Small, cheap items sell well in December, but fixed fees take a bigger bite out of them. Take a 4 oz travel tin:

4 oz tinCost
Materials (wax, fragrance, tin, wick, label)$2.10
Labour (8 min at $20/hr)$2.67
Overhead (10%)$0.48
Full cost$5.25

Price = ($5.25 + $0.45) ÷ 0.555 = $10.27, so list it at $10.99. Fees are $1.49 and it earns $4.25 a sale.

The $0.45 of fixed fees is about 4% of a $10.99 tin, but only about 2% of a $19.99 candle. That's why pricing a mini at "half the big one" usually goes wrong. Price it with the formula, then consider selling minis mainly inside gift sets, where the fixed fees are paid once.

6. Plan your stock around the order you can actually fulfil

Seasonal profit is also about not overcommitting. Before you list December offers:

  1. Count your real capacity. If a gift set takes 27 minutes, ten orders a day is 4.5 hours of packing before you make anything new.
  2. Make the sets in batches. Pouring 20 candles in one session usually cuts minutes per candle. Re-cost the labour line if it does.
  3. Set a last order date for your slowest shipping option and show it clearly in your listings. That's honest urgency: a real date, not a fake countdown.
  4. Order supplies early. Jar and wax prices can rise or go out of stock in the rush. If your costs change, re-run the formula before you re-list.

In January: review, don't guess

Once the rush is over, compare what each offer actually earned. Look at profit per sale and profit per hour, not just revenue. Keep the sets and codes that paid off and retire the ones that didn't. Then put any price changes in place before spring, rather than reacting mid-season.

December pricing checklist

  • Every gift set costed as its own product, box and card included
  • Discount codes checked against profit per sale, not just price
  • Shipping you absorb counted as a cost
  • Market pitch fees and travel spread across expected sales
  • No product sold below full cost, unless it's a deliberate, counted promotion
  • Prices re-checked if supplier costs changed this year

Frequently asked questions

Should gift sets be cheaper than buying the items separately?

Slightly, if the set saves you work. Pass on part of the saving (one pack instead of two) and keep the rest. Don't discount below the set's own full cost plus fees.

What's a sensible holiday discount?

There's no single right number. Work out profit per sale at the discounted price and how many extra sales you'd need. If you can't realistically make that many, choose a gift set or a small extra instead.

Do Etsy fees apply to shipping?

Etsy's transaction fee applies to the shipping you charge as well as the item price, which is one more reason to cost free shipping into the price rather than give it away blindly.
