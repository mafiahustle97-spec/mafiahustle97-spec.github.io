Bookkeeping for Etsy Sellers: A Simple 15-Minute Weekly System

Bookkeeping for a small Etsy shop isn't about accounting jargon. It's three habits: record every sale with its fees, record every expense with a receipt, and check once a month that it all matches the money in your account. Here's a system that takes about 15 minutes a week.

Most makers put off bookkeeping because it feels like a big, technical job. For a small shop it isn't. The work is mostly copying numbers you already have into one place, regularly, so that at the end of the year you have a clean record instead of a shoebox.

This guide covers what to record, where Etsy keeps the numbers, how to make a payout make sense, and a weekly routine you can actually stick to.

Not advice

This is general information about keeping organised records. What you must record, and how it's taxed, depends on your country. A qualified accountant can tell you what applies to you.

The two logs every shop needs

Everything in small-shop bookkeeping comes from two lists.

1. A sales log, one row per order:

Expense categories that fit a handmade shop

Use a short, fixed list so every month is comparable. These eighteen cover most makers:

  • Materials & Supplies and Packaging (the things that go into or around a product)
  • Shipping Supplies & Postage not tied to a specific order
  • Platform & Listing Fees not on a sale (Etsy Ads, Etsy Plus, extra listing renewals)
  • Advertising & Marketing, Software & Subscriptions, Equipment & Tools
  • Studio Rent & Utilities, Craft Fair & Booth Fees, Photography & Props
  • Professional Services, Education & Training, Travel, Bank & Payment Fees
  • Insurance, Office Supplies, Samples & Giveaways, Other

Use Other sparingly. If you notice the same thing landing there every month, give it a category.

For anything used partly for personal life (a phone, a home internet line, a car), record the business-use percentage. Whether and how much of it counts as a business expense is a question for your accountant, but the record should exist.

Where to find the numbers in Etsy

You don't need to type orders from memory. In your Etsy Shop Manager:

  • Orders CSV. Settings → Options → Download Data lets you download an orders file for a month. It lists each order with item totals and shipping.
  • Monthly statement. Finances → Monthly statements shows sales, fees by type (transaction, processing, listing, Offsite Ads), shipping labels and refunds.
  • Payments / deposits. Finances → Payment account shows each payout to your bank.

Menu names change from time to time, so if you can't find one, search Etsy's Help Center for "download data" or "monthly statement".

The orders file gives you the sales log. The statement gives you the fee totals to check against. Shipping labels bought through Etsy appear on the statement and are taken from your balance.

Reconciling an Etsy payout

The number that confuses most sellers is the deposit. Etsy pays out your balance after taking fees and any labels you bought, so the deposit never matches your sales. Here's a made-up week:

LineAmount
Gross sales (items + shipping charged)$412.60
Less: Etsy fees (transaction, processing, listings)−$45.83
Less: shipping labels bought on Etsy−$61.40
Less: refund to a customer−$29.95
Deposit to your bank$275.42

If you record the $275.42 deposit as "sales", your books under-report sales by $137.18 and your fees and postage vanish. Instead, record gross sales, fees, postage and refunds as separate lines. They should add up to the deposit. If they don't, something is missing: often an Offsite Ads fee, a currency conversion fee or an Etsy Ads charge.

This check, done monthly, is called reconciliation. It's the single habit that makes your books trustworthy.

The 15-minute weekly routine

Pick a fixed slot (Monday morning works for many shops) and do the same five things.

  • Sales (5 min): add last week's orders from every channel: Etsy, your own site, markets, Instagram. Paste from the orders CSV if you have many.
  • Expenses (4 min): add every business purchase, with category and business-use %.
  • Receipts (3 min): photograph or download each receipt into one folder named by month. Tick "receipt saved".
  • Refunds and pending (1 min): update the status of any refunded or unpaid orders.
  • Glance at the numbers (2 min): week's sales, anything odd, stock running low.

Then once a month, after the statement arrives:

  • Reconcile payouts against your sales log (the table above)
  • Check fee totals against the statement and correct any sale that differs
  • Read the month's P&L
  • Move your tax set-aside to a separate account (see the tax set-aside guide)

Five habits that save the most pain

  1. Use a separate account for the business. Even a second personal account used only for the shop makes the expense log almost automatic and reconciliation much easier.
  2. Record gross, not net. Sales before fees, fees as their own line. Never just the payout.
  3. Keep receipts as you go. A receipt photographed today takes ten seconds. Finding it next January takes an afternoon, if it's findable at all.
  4. Log every channel. Market cash sales and Instagram orders are sales too. If they aren't in the log, your profit and your tax records are wrong.
  5. Close each month. Bookkeeping that's three months behind is a project. Bookkeeping that's one week behind is a habit.

Year-end checklist

  • All twelve months reconciled to Etsy statements and your bank
  • Every expense has a category and a receipt
  • Stock counted, if you use the cost-of-goods method (see cost of goods for handmade products)
  • Equipment bought this year listed separately (it may be treated differently for tax)
  • Year's P&L saved as a PDF along with the year's statements
  • Set-aside total compared with what you actually owe, once known
  • A fresh copy of your workbook set up for the new year

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to do bookkeeping if my Etsy shop is small?

Records are useful at any size, and in many countries you're expected to keep them once you're selling regularly. Check what applies where you live. The routine above takes about 15 minutes a week for a small shop.

Can I just use my Etsy statements as my books?

They're an essential source, but they only cover Etsy. Market, website and Instagram sales and almost all your expenses happen elsewhere. A simple log brings it together.

What if I've already fallen months behind?

Start with this week, so the habit begins now. Then catch up one month at a time, oldest first, using the Etsy orders download and your bank statements.

Should I record sales tax Etsy collects?

In many US states Etsy collects and remits sales tax as a marketplace facilitator, and it isn't your income. It may appear on your statement, so note it, but keep it out of gross sales. Ask an accountant if you also sell elsewhere.
